Back to School

The team-uniform timeline: how to work backwards from your first game

By Kristen Coats · July 29, 2026

Every August the same phone call comes in: “First game is Friday and my jerseys aren’t here.” Sometimes we can rescue it, sometimes we can’t. So let me save you the panic — here’s the timeline I wish every coach, booster, and school secretary had on their fridge.

You don’t plan a uniform order forward from the day you think of it. You plan it backwards from your first game or first day of school. Every step has a real, non-negotiable lead time. Miss one, and the whole thing tips over.

The full timeline, working backwards

Here’s what a healthy team uniform order actually looks like, counting back from the day you need them in hand. This is for a normal 20–40 piece order — one logo, one or two colors, standard blanks in stock.

  • Day 0 — first game / first day. You need the uniforms in your hands, distributed, and on kids.
  • Day −3 — pickup or delivery. Give yourself a 3-day cushion for handing out, swapping the “this XL is actually a large” trades, and dealing with any missing name.
  • Day −7 to −10 — production. Once you’ve approved the proof, actual embroidery or DTF printing runs 5–10 business days on a normal order. Bigger orders, rush of last-minute back-to-school work, or a color match that has to be dialed in can push it. Peak August is the tightest window we run all year.
  • Day −12 to −14 — proof approval + roster lock. This is where orders quietly die. I send you a digital proof (free, no commitment) showing the logo mocked up on the exact garment, at the exact size, in the exact thread colors. You have to actually look at it and reply. If it goes to a booster committee that meets on Tuesdays, that’s a week gone right there. Lock your roster and sizes at the same moment you approve the proof — every time somebody remembers a kid two days later, the whole timeline slips.
  • Day −16 to −20 — logo digitizing (first-time orders only). If you’ve never had your logo embroidered with us before, we have to digitize it — that’s the process of converting your art file into stitch instructions. It’s a one-time step (we keep the file), takes 1–3 business days, and it’s where surprise delays come from if you send in a low-res logo scraped off Facebook. Send a clean vector file (.ai, .eps, .svg, or a high-res .png on transparent background) and you’re fine.
  • Day −21 — order placed, blanks confirmed. This is when you actually say “go.” At this point I place the blank-goods order (polos, tees, hats, whatever the substrate is), confirm color and size availability, and we know for sure the goods will land in time. The blanks are the real bottleneck — a specific color of a specific brand of polo can be backordered nationally, and no amount of hustle from my machines fixes that. Ordering three weeks out means I can swap to an equivalent in stock if something’s out.

Add that up: from the day you say “let’s do this” to the first game, you want roughly three full weeks. Four is comfortable. Two is doable but you’re eating your safety margin. One week is a rescue mission and I have to say no more often than I say yes.

What to hand your uniform person before they call

If you’re the coach or booster in charge of this year’s order, you can cut a week off the timeline just by walking in with the right stuff. Here’s the checklist:

  • Vector logo — .ai, .eps, .svg, or a high-res .png on a transparent background. A screenshot of your Instagram profile is not a logo file. If all you have is a picture, we can usually redraw it, but that’s an extra day and a small fee.
  • Exact roster with sizes. Kid names, adult sizes (S, M, L, XL, 2XL — actual sizes, not “small-ish”), and a spare of each in case somebody joins the team the week of the first game. Nobody has ever regretted ordering two extra mediums.
  • Color decisions locked. Jersey color, logo colors, thread colors. If your school colors are “royal blue and gold” — great, but there are eight royal blues out there. Bring a swatch or a picture of last year’s shirts and I’ll match them.
  • Names or numbers. If jerseys need player names or numbers, get me that list at the same time as the sizes. Personalization adds a small amount to production time and cost per piece — worth knowing up front.
  • Budget per piece. Tell me the number you can spend per shirt or per hat. I’ll steer you to the right blank instead of the fanciest one, and we can usually hit whatever you need.

When you’re already late

Sometimes I get the call the week before. Here’s what actually helps and what doesn’t.

Helps: Being flexible on the exact garment. If you were dreaming of a specific Nike polo but I can get you an equally good in-stock alternative today, the timeline suddenly works. Also — DTF printing is faster to run than embroidery, so for a rush shirt job we might switch methods.

Doesn’t help: Adding pieces mid-run, arguing with the fabric warehouse, or emailing your logo in ten different file formats on ten different days. Pick one file, one roster, one color, and let us go.

If you’re staring down a deadline right now, don’t guess whether we can make it. Text me the date and I’ll tell you honestly whether it’s doable or not, and if not, what we can do instead. That’s a two-minute conversation and it beats finding out on Thursday.
