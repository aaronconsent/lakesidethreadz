Medical & Professional

The clinic staff uniform playbook: embroidered scrubs, polos, and lab coats without the reorder chaos

By Kristen Coats · August 1, 2026

Most of the clinics and dental offices we work with around Lake Livingston don’t want a uniform program. They want their front desk to look pulled together, their hygienists in matching scrubs, and a couple of embroidered lab coats for the providers. Then they want it to stay that way — even when someone new starts in October, or a size doesn’t fit, or the last batch mysteriously walked out of the break room.

That “stay that way” part is where most small-clinic apparel orders quietly fall apart. Here’s what actually works, from someone who runs the machines.

Pick the pieces before you pick the logo placement

The mistake I see most often is a clinic ordering one style of polo for the whole staff and discovering three weeks later that it fits the front desk fine but is miserable for the hygienist who’s bending over patients all day. Different roles need different pieces. Sort them up front:

  • Front desk / office staff. A structured polo or a soft-woven button-down. Left chest embroidery of the clinic logo, roughly 3.5 inches wide. Charcoal, navy, or black hides the coffee.
  • Hygienists, techs, and MAs. Scrub tops (usually V-neck or mock-wrap) in whatever color code your clinic already uses. Embroidered left chest, same 3.5-inch logo. Skip the back print — it wears out from the chair.
  • Providers (MD, DO, DDS, NP, PA). Embroidered lab coat or a heavier consultation jacket. Logo on the left chest, name and credentials underneath in a matching thread. This is the piece patients photograph, so it’s worth spending the extra $15 per piece to get a real lab coat instead of the cheapest one on the site.
  • Sterile processing, back-of-house. Cheaper poly-cotton scrub tops, DTF-printed logo instead of embroidered. These take abuse and get replaced every 6–9 months anyway — no reason to pay for stitching.

Deciding this before you send us a logo means we can quote you accurately and you’re not surprised when the lab coat quote comes back at $65 and the scrub top comes back at $22.

Thread color, name embroidery, and the reorder problem

Two decisions at the first order will save you two years of headaches.

Lock the thread colors and write them down. When we embroider your logo the first time, we pick a specific thread number for every color in it — Madeira 1800 for a certain navy, Madeira 1051 for a certain gold, and so on. Those numbers get saved in your file. When you reorder in six months, or when you add a new hire in a year, we pull the same file and the logo comes out identical. Ask us for that list. Put it in the same folder as your logo files. If you ever change embroidery shops, hand them that list and you skip a whole round of “which navy did we use again?”

Decide the name-embroidery format on day one. First name only? First name plus credentials? First initial plus last name? Whatever you pick, stick with it. The clinic that decides in August that everyone gets “Dr. Smith, DDS” and in November wants to switch to “Bob” now has two versions of the same lab coat in circulation, and the staff photo looks off. Pick a format and add it to your onboarding checklist next to the parking pass.

Order two extras of every common size. M and L in scrubs, L in polos. When someone new starts, we can get their name added and get them a top out of your existing stock the same week — no waiting on a fresh order and no per-piece rush setup fee. The two extras cost you maybe $50 total. It’s the cheapest insurance in the whole program.

Realistic turnaround, and what “rush” actually means

A first-time embroidered scrub or polo order for a clinic runs about 10 to 14 business days from proof approval. That breaks down as:

  • 1–3 business days to digitize the logo if we don’t have it on file yet
  • 2–3 business days for you to look at the digital proof and reply
  • 5–10 business days of actual production, depending on quantity and what else is on the machines
  • 1–2 days of buffer for pickup or delivery

Reorders — same logo, same thread colors, same garment — are usually 5–7 business days because the digitizing is done and we’re just running the machine.

“Rush” in the embroidery world doesn’t mean overnight. It means we move your job in front of somebody else’s, and it usually adds a 20–35% rush fee. Even with rush, we still need the blanks to arrive from the supplier — which is why the fastest possible turnaround on a real clinic order, no matter what anyone promises, is about a week. If you want a lab coat embroidered for a new provider starting Monday, call us on Wednesday of the prior week at the latest.

What to send us to get a real quote

If you’re an office manager pricing this out, skip the “how much are polos” call. Send us:

  • The logo file (vector .ai, .eps, .svg, or a high-res .png on a transparent background)
  • A rough headcount by role — “6 front desk, 4 hygienists, 2 providers”
  • Any color your practice already uses (a paint chip, a picture of last year’s scrubs, or a Pantone number)
  • Whether you want names embroidered
